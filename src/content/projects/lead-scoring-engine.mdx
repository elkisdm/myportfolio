## The problem

Lead #51 never got read.

Not because it was bad — it might have been the best lead of the day. But by lead #50, the
agent had spent four hours reading WhatsApp messages, and #51 looked exactly like the 38%
that were unreachable anyway.

The inbox was receiving **800–1,200 inbound leads per week**. Four agents had to read each
one, ask for the address, manually check fibra coverage on three external portals, and decide
whether to follow up. Most quit reading after lead #50 of the day. I watched it happen.

Three things were broken:

1. **~4 hours/day of triage per agent.** Manual address parsing, three external portal lookups,
   judgment calls on incomplete info. Scoring was vibes.
2. **~38% of inbound leads were unreachable.** No fibra coverage at their address. Agents only
   found out after a 4-minute back-and-forth.
3. **Hot leads cooled in the queue.** A teletrabajo lead in Las Condes would sit behind 30
   unqualified messages. By the time an agent got there, the lead had already bought elsewhere.

## How I approached it

Speed-to-tier was the only metric that mattered. Everything else — model choice, cache strategy,
schema design — was downstream of *"how do we get an agent looking at a HOT lead in under 30 seconds?"*

### Feasibility before intent. Always.

If we can't deliver fibra to their door, nothing else matters. Feasibility check runs first —
costs 30 ms when cached, ~3 s on miss. Anything else (intent classification, behavioral lookup)
is skipped entirely on unfeasible leads. Saves ~38% of GPT calls outright.

### 60-day building-level cache.

Fibra coverage doesn't change daily — it changes when an operator turns on a new building. So
we cache by *normalized building address* (not full address), TTL 60 days. One dpto 803 lookup
serves dptos 101–1204. **87% hit rate within two weeks.**

### GPT-4o-mini, not 4o. With strict prompts.

For intent classification, mini hits **94% agreement with human agents at 1/15th the cost.**
The full 4o didn't move the needle on agreement; it just got more verbose. We trade flexibility
for latency and cost — and let prompts do the heavy lifting.

### Tier = signal, not a number.

No "lead score 87/100" — that invites debate and false precision. Three tiers (HOT / WARM /
COLD), each tied to a concrete agent action. HOT = call within 15 minutes. WARM = WhatsApp
follow-up today. COLD = automated nurture.

## What I'd do differently

Three honest ones:

1. **The intent prompt is a string literal.** It works. But every tweak needs a deploy. Should
   have versioned prompts as their own table from day one. Fixing in v2.
2. **Cache invalidation by TTL only.** When an operator turns on a new building, we wait up to
   60 days. A scraper for operator coverage feeds would solve it. Half-built already.
3. **No human-in-the-loop on misclassifications.** Agents flag bad tiers manually, but the
   corrections don't feed back into the model. A feedback loop could halve the 6% miss rate.
