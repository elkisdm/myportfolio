## The problem

"Where does the lead data live?"

Depends who you asked. Sales pointed at a Google Sheet. Ops pointed at two Notion databases. The follow-ups lived in a Trello board, and the proof of everything else was a screenshot somewhere in WhatsApp.

Every monthly close meant a week of reconciling those five sources. Agent performance was tracked by hand. Onboarding a new agent was a 2-hour walkthrough of "where things live."

The deal was one platform, built solo, in one quarter.

## How I approached it

**Boring stack on purpose.** Astro for delivery, Supabase for data + auth + RLS, TypeScript everywhere, Tailwind for styling, Vercel for deploy. Nothing exotic. No Redux, no GraphQL, no microservices. The point was reliability, not novelty.

**Schema first.** Spent the first week not writing UI. Just modeling: leads, agents, properties, conversations, factibilidad checks. Drew the RLS policies before writing a single component. The schema barely moved after that — additions, never reshapes.

**RBAC from day 1.** Three roles: admin, agent, viewer. RLS enforces it at the database level — not in the application layer. An agent literally cannot SELECT another agent's leads. Auditable, testable, hard to mess up.

**CSV import as a feature, not a hack.** Ops still receives some leads as CSVs from partners. Instead of asking them to stop, I built a CSV importer with column mapping, dedup, and a preview before commit.

## Outcome

Onboarded 12 agents in two weeks. Three spreadsheets, two Notion databases, and one Trello board retired. Monthly close went from a week to a day.

## What I'd do differently

The "performance" dashboard was overbuilt for what ops actually used. They cared about 4 numbers; I built 14. Strip-back is easier than build-up — next time I'll launch with the minimum and add by request.
